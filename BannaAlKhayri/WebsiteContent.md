*Updated monthly. Current entry:*

At the end of a road in Rafah, eleven households were sharing one working water point. Twenty minutes there, twenty minutes back, three times a day, and mostly it was the children carrying it.

### FAQs — About Us

**Are you a religious organisation?**
No. Our charitable objects are humanitarian, and we serve people on the basis of assessed need alone. Many of our supporters give in fulfilment of Islamic obligations, and we account for that giving properly and separately — that is a service we provide to donors, not a purpose of the charity.

**Do you take a political position?**
No. We describe humanitarian conditions and our humanitarian response. We do not campaign on political questions, attribute blame for the conflict, or align with any political actor. Charities may not have political purposes, and in our operating environment this is a practical necessity as well as a principle.

**Are you an intermediary — do you pass donations to another organisation?**
No. We deliver through our own field operation and formally contracted implementing partners, and we retain accountability for the end use of every pound. Where a partner delivers, we remain responsible.

**You are a young charity. Why should I trust you?**
We completed our first full year of operations in July 2026, so we are asking you to take something on trust, and we know it. Being this size is why we can still account for projects one at a time rather than in aggregate. Read one of the reports and see whether it tells you what you would want to know.

**How much of my donation goes on running the charity?**
Some of it does, and we would rather say so than let you assume otherwise. Our operating costs pay for the financial controls, monitoring, safeguarding, partner due diligence, insurance, examination and reporting that make our work verifiable. # 3.2 OUR STORY

**SEO page title:** Our Story | Banna Al Khayri Relief & Development
**SEO meta description:** Why Banna Al Khayri was established, what registration as a CIO required of us, what proved harder than expected, and what changed as a result.
**URL:** `/about/our-story`

---

### HERO

## We started with a food parcel. The hard part came afterwards.

---

### THE BEGINNING

*From Lucky Panna, Founder and Chair of Trustees.*

A friend gave me a hundred pounds for Gaza. A few weeks later she asked what it had bought.

I could not tell her. Not because anything had gone wrong — as far as I know it did exactly what it was meant to. But there was nothing to look at. No list, no receipt, no name. Money had left, something had arrived, and the middle belonged to nobody.

She was not asking to check up on me. She was asking because she wanted to picture it. That is what most people want. And she had given from an income that did not have a hundred pounds spare in it.

We chose the name *Banna Al Khayri* — builder of good — before there was anything to attach it to. It was closer to an intention than a name.

---

### WHY WE REGISTERED

We could have carried on informally. A great deal of generosity moves that way, and much of it does real good.

We registered because informal giving cannot answer the question my friend asked. It cannot produce a set of accounts. It cannot show a distribution list. It cannot show that a family was chosen because they needed it most, rather than because somebody knew their cousin. And there is nobody it can be held to account by.

Registering as a Charitable Incorporated Organisation meant taking on duties: a registered purpose that limits what we are allowed to do, trustees who carry personal responsibility for it, accounts anyone may inspect, and a regulator entitled to ask us anything.

Those duties are not the price of the work. They are how we answer the question.

---

### THE FIRST YEAR

Our first full financial year ran to 31 July 2026.

In it we sent water by tanker to households whose supply had stopped. We distributed family food parcels and fresh vegetable baskets. We funded communal cooking, so that families with nothing left to cook on could still eat something hot. We put up tents for families sleeping without a roof. And we began paying monthly support directly to the grandparents, aunts and older siblings raising orphaned children.

Every one of those projects has a file — the purchase record, the distribution list, the reconciliation, the photographs, and the report that followed.

---

### WHAT PROVED HARDER THAN WE EXPECTED

Two things, and I would rather you heard them from us.

**Getting money in.** Moving funds into Gaza is the hardest thing we do. Harder than buying, harder than distributing. Ordinary banking is frequently closed to organisations working there, and financial institutions can withdraw services from a charity like ours with little notice and no obligation to explain. So we have had to build documented routes, hold them to the same standard of evidence a bank would, and be able to show a compliance officer who has never met us where every pound came from and where it went. It is slow, expensive and unglamorous, and there is no way around it.

**Governance is not something you write once.** We commissioned a full review of our policies in 2026, and it found real problems. Documents inherited from templates that did not fit our legal form. Standards promised in one policy and contradicted in another. And a counter-terrorism policy which, when somebody finally read it properly, turned out to contain no counter-terrorism content at all.

We withdrew it. We are rebuilding the whole suite from first principles and working through the corrections at every board meeting.

I mention it because it is true, and because I would rather you knew we look. The review was uncomfortable to read. It is also the most useful thing we have done.

---

### WHERE WE ARE NOW

A small organisation. A working field team. A board that meets and minutes what it decides. A reporting standard we publish. And a governance programme that is in progress rather than finished.

I founded the charity and I chair its board, and I also carry a good deal of the day-to-day work. That combination is set out in full on our trustee page, together with how the board manages it and how we intend to reduce it over time. It is an ordinary situation in a young charity, and the right thing to do with it is to say so.

---

### WHERE WE ARE GOING

Deeper before wider.

I would like this to still be going in thirty years, long after any of us are involved. That means building things now that will still work at ten times the size — reporting that stays readable at several thousand records, financial coding that could hold more than one country, a board that grows into committees rather than into dependence on individuals.

Our purpose, as registered, is the relief of poverty and hardship among families displaced or affected by the conflict in the Occupied Palestinian Territories. Changing it requires the prior written consent of the Charity Commission, and until that is given we will not describe ourselves as doing anything else.

In the meantime there is a road in Rafah where the water arrives on Tuesdays and Fridays. That is the size of thing we are trying to do well.

# 4.1 HOW WE WORK

**SEO page title:** How We Work | From Your Donation to a Published Report | Banna Al Khayri
**SEO meta description:** The full chain, step by step: how a donation is allocated, transferred, procured, distributed, documented, verified, reconciled and reported. The answer to "does it actually get there?"
**URL:** `/our-work`

---

### HERO

## "Does it actually get there?"

People apologise for asking. Please don't. It is the right question, and the answer is not a promise — it is nine ordinary steps, each of which leaves something behind that can be checked.

---

### THE CHAIN

**1. You donate.**
You choose an appeal, a programme, or you leave it to our team to put where the need is greatest.

**2. Your donation is allocated.**
Every gift is coded to a fund the day it arrives. If you gave to a named appeal, your money is restricted to that purpose in law — not by our good intentions, but by law. We could not spend it elsewhere even if we wanted to.

**3. Funds are transferred through our documented route.**
This is the hardest part of the whole operation. Ordinary banking into Gaza is frequently unavailable, so we use routes our trustees have approved, with counterparties we have identified and screened, and we can show where every pound went. [How funds reach our work →]

**4. Goods are bought locally wherever we can.**
There are traders in Gaza who have reopened with half a shelf of stock and no certainty of restocking. Buying from them does three things at once: the family gets what it needs, the trader stays in business, and we get an invoice from a supplier we have assessed — which is what lets us reconcile the project afterwards. When local supply fails, and it does, we say so in the report.

**5. Our team distributes.**
Against a list built from household assessment, not from whoever happens to be standing there when the lorry arrives.

**6. The paperwork comes back.**
Supplier invoices. The distribution list, marked off by hand. Signed or witnessed receipts. Dated photographs, taken with consent. It is the least interesting part of the day and it is what makes everything else believable.

**7. Some distributions are checked independently.**
Someone who was not there goes back afterwards and confirms with households that it happened.

**8. The project is reconciled.**
Field spending is matched against receipts, purchase records and the distribution list — by someone other than the person who handled the money.

**9. We report on it.**
What was delivered, where, to how many households, over what period, at what cost — and what went wrong, if something did. We publish reports across our programmes and on the appeals we close.

**[ Read a project report ]**

---

### HOW WE CHOOSE WHO RECEIVES ASSISTANCE

Three families live on the same street. Two will receive a parcel this week and one will not, because there is not enough for all three. Somebody has to decide, and that decision is the heaviest thing our field team carries.

So we do not leave it to the day. Selection is made against documented criteria, applied consistently and written down:

- Household composition, and the number of dependants
- Displacement status, and how many times the household has moved
- Current access to water, food and shelter
- Presence of children, elderly people, or people with disabilities or chronic illness
- Whether the household already receives assistance from another source, so that we do not give twice to one family while their neighbour receives nothing

The assessment is recorded before the distribution, not written up afterwards. The list is then checked against what was actually handed over.

We never select on relationship, faith, politics or family connection. Our field staff have relatives in the streets they work in — of course they do, they live there. The criteria exist because that is true. They protect the third family, and they protect our team from being asked.

---

### OUR PARTNERS, AND HOW WE VET THEM

Where a partner delivers, we remain accountable for the outcome.

Before any funds move we assess the partner's legal standing, governance, financial controls, safeguarding practice and delivery record, and we screen the organisation and its principals against applicable sanctions and proscription requirements. Every partnership operates under a formal written agreement executed by at least two of our trustees.

**[ Our partners →]**

---

### MANAGING RISK IN A DIFFICULT ENVIRONMENT

Three things are non-negotiable, and none of them bends for operational convenience.

**Counter-terrorism and sanctions compliance.** Organisations proscribed under UK law must not receive funds, goods, services or any economic benefit from this charity, directly or indirectly. This is an absolute constraint. It is not subject to humanitarian override, operational necessity, local convention or anyone's good intentions. We screen partners and suppliers, verify the end use of funds, and identify and comply with any licence or exemption we rely on.

**Financial control.** Authorisation, payment, reconciliation and reporting sit with different people. Purchasing above set thresholds is competitive. Assets are recorded. Our trustees review the operation of these controls and report the results to the board.

**Safeguarding.** Everyone who works for us or on our behalf carries safeguarding obligations, with a route for raising a concern that does not pass through the person the concern might be about. [Safeguarding →]

We describe these controls in principle. We do not publish the level of detail that would help someone work out how to get round them.

---

### WHAT HAPPENS IF A PROJECT CANNOT PROCEED

Restricted funds stay with their purpose. They do not get quietly moved.

Where that purpose becomes genuinely impossible — a route closes, a supplier fails, circumstances change — we follow our published Restricted Funds Policy. That may mean holding the money until delivery becomes possible, contacting donors, or a recorded trustee decision to apply it to the closest equivalent purpose. Whichever it is, it is written down and it goes in the report.

Usually the delay is a delay rather than an ending. In March a water delivery was held up for nine days. The funds sat where they were, the team found another way round, and the water arrived on the fourteenth.

---

### FAQs

**How do I know the money gets there?**
Through the chain above rather than through reassurance: a documented transfer route, local purchasing with supplier invoices, distribution against an assessed list, paperwork returned, independent checks on some distributions, reconciliation, and a published report. Then read one of the reports and judge it for yourself.

**Can you actually get aid in?**
Sometimes, and not always. Access changes week to week, and we would be misleading you to say otherwise. What we can tell you is what we delivered, where and when — and what happened to the funds during the weeks we could not.

**How do you make sure aid does not reach the wrong people?**
Documented beneficiary selection, verification at the point of distribution, screening of partners and suppliers, and documented end use of funds. Compliance here is not treated as optional in this organisation. We do not publish the operational detail of screening, for reasons we hope are obvious.

**Who is actually in charge?**
A board of trustees, with published interests, a defined scheme of delegation and named officer roles. [Meet the trustees →]

**What happens if something goes wrong?**
There is a complaints route with a published response standard, a separate whistleblowing route, an incident framework, and a commitment to report serious incidents to the Charity Commission. [Complaints & Concerns →]

**How much goes on running the charity?**
Some of it. Our operating costs pay for the controls, monitoring, safeguarding, due diligence, insurance, examination and reporting that make everything on this page verifiable. We publish the allocation and we publish our accounts. [Financial Transparency →]

### WHY THESE PAGES EXIST

Every Ramadan somebody emails to ask whether their Zakat will reach a household that is genuinely eligible. It is a careful question and it deserves a careful answer.

Someone fulfilling an obligation is in a different position from someone making a gift. If you give Sadaqah and it is spent a little differently from how you pictured, the good is still done. If you give Zakat and it reaches a household that was not eligible to receive it, your obligation has not been discharged — and in all likelihood nobody will ever tell you.

That is why these pages go into the detail they do. We treat Islamic giving as a category with rules attached, and we have built the bookkeeping to match.

---

### HOW WE HANDLE FAITH-BASED GIVING

**We ask you at the point of giving.** Not afterwards, and not by guessing from which appeal you chose. If you tell us it is Zakat, our records say Zakat.

**We account for it separately.** Zakat income and Zakat spending are coded so the two can be set against each other. That is what turns *"was my Zakat applied properly?"* into a question with an answer from the books.

**We apply it only to eligible households.** Eligibility is assessed household by household by our field team, against written criteria, and recorded before the distribution.

**We publish the policy.** Our Zakat Policy is a dated, version-controlled document you can read in full — what it funds, what it does not, who advised on it and when it was last looked at.

**We report.** Including on Qurbani and Zakat al-Fitr, which are the easiest to forget about once Eid has passed.

### A NOTE ON WHAT WE ARE

Our charitable objects are humanitarian. We are not a religious organisation and we do not describe ourselves as one — partly because it would misstate our entry on the public register, and partly because it would not be true.

What we are is a charity that has taken the trouble to handle faith-based giving properly, for supporters to whom it matters a great deal. It is a service we offer. It is not our purpose, and we hold that distinction deliberately.

We serve people on the basis of need, without distinction of faith or background, and we welcome support from people of every faith and none.

---

### WE DO NOT ISSUE RULINGS

These pages explain how each category is commonly understood and how we handle it. They are not a fatwa and should not be treated as one.

Where scholars hold different positions we say so, rather than picking one and presenting it as settled. And where your circumstances are complicated — as many people's are — please ask your own scholar. We would much rather you did that than relied on a charity's website.

### HERO

## A grandmother is already raising this child. We help her do it.

Sometimes it is an aunt, or an uncle, or a sister not much older herself. Whoever it is took the child in without hesitating and without any more income to do it with. Sponsorship is £50 a month, and it goes straight to them.

---

### WHAT THE PROGRAMME IS

A child who has lost a parent in Gaza is almost never alone. Somebody has taken them in — a surviving parent, a grandmother, an aunt, an older brother or sister. Nobody debated it. The household simply became larger overnight, and the money in it did not.

Our sponsorship programme provides **£50 per child per month**, paid as direct cash support to the guardian.

---

### WHY DIRECT CASH, AND NOT A PARCEL

Three reasons, and they are all practical.

**The household knows better than we do.** One family needs shoes so a child can go back to lessons. Another needs medicine. Another needs to clear a debt that is about to cost them the roof they are under. A standard parcel meets none of those.

**It keeps money in the neighbourhood.** What the family spends locally keeps those shops open, which matters to everyone living around them.

**It respects the guardian.** Someone raising their sister's children does not need a stranger in another country deciding what their household requires this month.

---

### HOW CHILDREN ARE IDENTIFIED AND VERIFIED

Children are identified through community-level assessment by our field team, verified against documentation where it exists and through corroborated community verification where it does not — which, after repeated displacement, is often the case. Papers are among the first things a family loses.

Each child on the register has a record: family circumstances, guardian, location, verification method and date. The register is reviewed periodically, because circumstances change.

We currently have **52 children on the sponsorship register**. **[VERIFY]**

---

### WHAT SPONSORS RECEIVE

An update on the child you are supporting at least once a year, covering how the child is, how the household is managing, and how the support has been used.

If the child's circumstances change — a move, a change of guardian, a family becoming self-sufficient, or a family we can no longer reach — we tell you and explain what happens next.

---

### SAFEGUARDING: WHAT WE DO AND DO NOT DO

**We do not facilitate direct contact between sponsors and children.** No addresses, no phone numbers, no direct messaging, no visits.

We know this disappoints people, and it comes from a kind impulse. It is still not something we will do. A bereaved child cannot be asked to manage a relationship with an adult they have never met, in another country, on whom their household's income depends. That imbalance is exactly what safeguarding exists to prevent, and good intentions on the sponsor's part do not remove it.

**We do not display children as a catalogue.** You cannot look through photographs and pick one. Matching is done by our field team, on the basis of need and the order in which sponsorship becomes available. A child set out to be chosen from has been made into something to select, and donor preference does not outweigh that.

**Everyone in the chain operates under safeguarding obligations**, with a reporting route that does not pass through the person a concern might be about.

---

### WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A CHILD'S CIRCUMSTANCES CHANGE

Children do leave the programme, and not always sadly. A family gets back on its feet. A guardian moves to be near relatives we cannot reach. A child reaches an age at which the programme no longer applies.

When it happens we tell the sponsor, explain as much as we properly can, and offer to move the sponsorship to another child. We do not keep taking a monthly payment for a child who is no longer on the register, and we do not quietly swap one child for another and say nothing.

---

### WHERE THE MONEY GOES

£50 per child per month reaches the guardian. The costs of running the programme — verification, field visits, record-keeping, safeguarding oversight, transmission and reporting — are met from our general funds and disclosed in our accounts.

We are not going to tell you a sponsorship programme costs nothing to run. It costs something, and what it buys is the confidence that the child is real, the guardian is who they say they are, and the money arrived in their hands.

### WHAT UNRESTRICTED GIVING IS

A household loses its shelter in a week when our open appeal happens to be for water. Our team can see the family. We may have money in the building. And we cannot use it, because that money was given for water and it belongs to water.

That is not a loophole. It is the law protecting your instruction, and we would not want it any other way.

Unrestricted giving is the money our trustees and field team can place wherever the need is greatest on the day. It is what closes the gap between one appeal and the next.

---

### WHAT IT ACTUALLY PAYS FOR

We want to be specific, because "where most needed" can mean anything if nobody explains it.

**The family who does not fit this month's appeal.** The one our team can see and would otherwise have to walk past.

**Speed.** Confirming an order before an appeal is fully funded, which is often the difference between delivering inside a window and missing it.

**The work that makes everything else checkable.** Financial controls. Monitoring. Independent checks on distributions. Due diligence on our partners. Safeguarding. Insurance. Independent examination of our accounts. The reports we publish.

That last group is our operating cost, and this is where it is met.

---

### A DIRECT ANSWER ON RUNNING COSTS

We do not claim that none of your donation is spent on running the charity. Every organisation that delivers aid responsibly has running costs, and an organisation that tells you its costs are zero has told you that governance, reporting and compliance are worthless.

Ours pay for the things that make our work verifiable. Monitoring a distribution is what allows us to confirm it happened. Due diligence on a partner is what prevents funds being misused. Publishing a report is what allows you to see the outcome. An organisation with none of those costs would have none of those assurances to offer.

We publish how donations are allocated, we keep our costs proportionate and justifiable, and we publish our annual accounts in full.
